A short evidence lab for ages 12+

Probability Detective

Predict, experiment, compare, and explain. Randomness can vary in a short run; probability describes the model, not a promise about the next result.

Activity 1 of 3

Coin laboratory

Choose a heads probability, predict, then run 10, 100, or 1,000 trials. The theoretical chance stays fixed while observed frequency moves around.

Hint: a fair coin's theoretical chance remains 1/2 even after a run of heads. A larger sample often gets closer, but it does not have to improve every time.

Dice investigation

Roll two fair six-sided dice. Single-die faces are equally likely, but sums in the middle have more combinations.

Fair-game designer

Build a points game. Player A wins with the chosen chance; B wins otherwise. Fairness here means equal expected points, not equal results in every run.

Check the arithmetic: A expected points = chance × A points; B expected points = (1 − chance) × B points.

Evidence notebook

Run each activity and record one observation and one limitation. Your conclusion should name evidence, then explain what the experiment cannot prove.

This is a model with documented random sampling. It teaches reasoning about chance; it is not gambling, a prediction service, or a guarantee of outcomes.
